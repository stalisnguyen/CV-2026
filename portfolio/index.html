Why I build

Retention is a product decision. So I build products.

People rarely drop a product for lack of features. They drop it when the next step is unclear, progress feels invisible and nothing brings them back.

So I build products that show the next step, remember what users did and make it easy to keep going. Five of them are built around that: Echolis, an IFS-inspired reflection app, and four AI learning web apps. It's Lifecycle & Platform Strategy applied to product.

How I build

One process for all nine products. I own every step and work with AI agents on content, code and tests.

  1. Define the userWho they are, what they need and how progress is measured.
  2. Spec with guardrailsAnswer validators and safety rules. Nothing ships until it passes.
  3. Build leanOne shared engine for the learning apps. One web app that also runs on iPhone.
  4. Test on phone & desktopKey flows end to end. Answers and safety cases re-checked automatically.
  5. Ship & iterateRead the logs and fix what real use reveals.

"You are not one voice. You are a conversation." 💬 Inner conflict, not a cheerleader

Most AI companions offer one agreeable bot. Echolis splits what users write across their inner parts, which reply to each other, so they see the conflict instead of just being soothed.

❓ Asks, doesn't advise

The Observer ends each reflection with one question, not a fix or a verdict, so the insight work stays with the user instead of depending on the app.

🧠 Memory users control

Parts remember earlier sessions and patterns surface over time. Users can see and delete their memories.

📊 Designed for retention

Built with a lifecycle mindset, with a weekly product scorecard (retention, active writers) and lifecycle messaging designed in.

📝 Reflect

Write freely or follow a guided start. The parts that are present answer in their own voices, and the Observer closes with one question.

🧶 Untangle a reaction

A step-by-step trace for a reaction that felt too big: trigger, part, what it protects and what it needs.

⚖️ Weigh a decision

For a choice that keeps circling. Every part with a stake speaks before the user decides.

💬 Talk to one part

A one-to-one conversation with a single part, to answer it with awareness instead of reflex.

🧩 Patterns

Pattern cards built from the user's reflections: trigger, protective response, hidden cost and what is already moving.

🌱 Emergent parts

A new part, such as the Inner Critic or the Caretaker, can surface from the user's own writing. They confirm it or say "not me".

🧠 Memory across sessions

Parts remember earlier sessions. Users can see and delete their memories in Settings.

🕰️ History

A timeline of past reflections, with the option to pick up an earlier session where it left off.

🔔 Gentle reminders

A daily reminder at a chosen time, plus a day-3 nudge for anyone who hasn't written yet.

📤 Export & delete

Users can download their story and reflections at any time, or delete their account.

👋 Try before signing up

One reflection on the web with no account, to meet the parts first.

For practitioners
Alongside work with clients

Built for IFS-trained therapists and coaches. A separate practice workspace that gives clients somewhere to reflect between sessions, and gives the practitioner a summary before they meet.

  • Practice dashboard with a Clients tab, in its own account type.
  • Client-led connection: the client creates an invite code in their own account, and the practitioner redeems it to connect.
  • Summaries, never raw writing: briefs show pattern signals and neutral session summaries, not what the client actually wrote.
  • The client decides what the practitioner sees: they can pause sharing, choose session by session, preview exactly what the practitioner sees, and disconnect, which removes access straight away.
  • "New since the last visit" is marked for each client.
  • Practice accounts are reviewed by hand before the badge appears.

Coming soon A "what changed since we last met" brief, private notes for each client, and a between-session prompt the practitioner can send for the client to sit with.

Safety decisionsage floor, crisis handling, replies
  • 16+ age floor, the same across the app, the Terms and the App Store rating, with extra safeguards for 16–17-year-olds.
  • Crisis detection tested on single messages and on crises that build gradually. It points people to their local emergency or hotline numbers, while ordinary venting doesn't set it off.
  • Shame and fear replies reassure first, use the person's own words, offer at most one small next step, and never diagnose.

Stalong 思龙

Mandarin Chinese, HSK 1 to 7–9 · for Vietnamese speakers
LIVE

A Mandarin course for Vietnamese speakers that runs from HSK 1 to HSK 7–9 on the HSK 3.0 standard. It uses Sino-Vietnamese (Hán Việt) readings as a memory bridge, and Tiểu Long, a baby dragon mascot, guides the way.

  • Smart flashcards: spaced repetition with tone-coloured pinyin, Hán Việt readings, Vietnamese meanings and example sentences, plus a searchable dictionary by hanzi, toneless pinyin, Hán Việt or Vietnamese.
  • Full curriculum: stroke-order writing practice, the 214 radicals, grammar notes flagging mistakes Vietnamese learners often make, and HSK mock tests for each level with scoring and explanations.
  • Speak it out loud: scenario-based AI conversations with corrections in Vietnamese, and shadowing practice that records the learner, transcribes their speech and compares it with the model sentence.
  • Habit loop: imperial-exam titles from Đồng sinh to Trạng nguyên, streaks, review games, a mistake log and a weekly group leaderboard.

Catalis

IELTS Academic & General Training · band 4.5 to 9
LIVE

IELTS prep for Vietnamese learners, from band 4.5 to 9, coached by Jack, an orange cat the app also calls Mít. It is built around honest progress, so it never gives a flattering band score.

  • Objective placement: an adaptive vocabulary test with grammar, timed reading and optional listening, weighted and rounded down. Band estimates are split by skill, and the app says plainly when it doesn't have enough data yet.
  • Plan that fits the day: a daily route built from the target band and minutes per day. Listening uses a distinct natural voice for each speaker plus dictation, and the full 40-question Reading mocks convert to a band.
  • AI feedback: Writing and Speaking scored against the four IELTS criteria, with Speaking recorded on phone or desktop. An AI room lets learners debate, practise paraphrasing and turn real English text into flashcards.
  • Habit loop: titles from Freshman to Professor, streaks, daily tips, pronunciation drills, games and a mistake log.

Calculis

GMAT Focus Edition · from the basics to 705+
LIVE

GMAT Focus Edition prep covering Quant, Verbal and Data Insights, for complete beginners and 705+ targets alike. Max the fox is the study buddy and AI tutor.

  • Exam-real practice: all 8 question types, including sortable Table Analysis, SVG-drawn charts, multi-tab Multi-Source Reasoning and an on-screen calculator for Data Insights. There are 5 difficulty levels, timed modes, and a worked solution with trap notes for every question.
  • Adaptive mocks: a full 64-question exam with a choice of section order, a 10-minute break and Review & Edit for up to 3 answers. There is also a 15-question diagnostic.
  • Transparent scoring: section (60–90) and total (205–805) estimates from a 3-parameter IRT model, clearly labelled as an estimate rather than GMAC's algorithm.
  • Learning loop: an 81-step "Start here" path and an error log that tags why each question was missed. Max gives up to 3 step-by-step hints without revealing the answer. Titles run from Intern to CEO.

Neuralis

AI & Robotics from zero · career paths
LIVE

Learn AI and robotics from zero, deep enough to understand the field and build a career in it: AI engineering, forward-deployed engineering, robotics or AI product. Lessons are explained in Vietnamese with English terminology, and Bit the robot keeps learners company.

Cellis

Cell architecture · Vietnamese & English
LIVEIN PROGRESS

An interactive guide to how a cell is built. Sixteen 3D specimens cover basic cells, bacteria, organelles, cells in the human body, viruses and DNA, in Vietnamese and English, under the line “Explore the architecture of life.”

  • Sixteen specimens: a plant cell and an animal cell, cyanobacteria and Salmonella, a mitochondrion and a chloroplast, red and white blood cells, a neuron, skeletal muscle, sperm and an egg, plus influenza, coronavirus, a bacteriophage and DNA.
  • Labels on the model: the nucleus, the mitochondrion and the cell membrane are marked on the 3D view. A side panel covers the structures, what they do, a glossary and a longer note.
  • Tour, quiz and compare: on the animal cell, a guided tour follows one protein out of the cell, in six steps. A quiz asks which part is blinking. A table sets the plant cell, the animal cell and a cyanobacterium side by side.
  • Search, and a video frame: a search box for parts and terms. Video mode runs a vertical 9:16 frame, with replay and exit.

The Fifth Watch · Đại Việt, 1442 · English & Vietnamese
LIVEIN PROGRESS

An early test build of a history game in the browser. Language is chosen on the first screen, and the visuals are built in code. Emperor Lê Thái Tông dies suddenly at Lệ Chi Garden, and the court annals record one line: "Everyone says Nguyễn Thị Lộ killed the Emperor." The player takes three roles, each holding one piece of the rumor, to find who said it first.

  • Three roles, one rumor: a court historian, a palace maid and an imperial physician. Each sees only part of it. Playing all three is what puts the story together.

Pastelux

Architectural lighting workspace · English & Vietnamese
LIVE

A bilingual workspace for architects and lighting designers. English and Vietnamese sit side by side, under the line "Architectural lighting, stay current." The morning digest opens onto guides, a standards comparison, calculators, a drawing board and an assistant called Stas.

  • Calculators, with the drawing: beam angle to spot size, drawn as a live section through the beam; a lumen method for the room; the inverse square law; melanopic EDI from vertical lux at the eye and the spectrum. An IES or LDT file opens as a polar curve, and a fixture schedule totals watts and W/m².
  • Board, Bảng nháp: a canvas for a sketch, a moodboard or a ceiling plan. Templates start a lighting concept in layers or a ceiling sheet, with lighting symbols, notes, lines and frames. Stas can be asked about the board on screen.
  • Standards, side by side: maintained illuminance, glare, uniformity and colour rendering by space type. EN 12464-1 sits next to typical IES practice and TCVN 7114-1, with a diagram of the task, the surround and the background. The figures are marked indicative, to be checked against the standard the contract names.
  • Ask Stas, and the shelf around it: questions on the work or on lighting, with a picture attached. Around that, guides on building type, circadian design, colour past CRI, control protocols, dark-sky façades and the stages of a commission, plus case studies, a glossary, a directory and a calendar of fairs.

Agent Radar

Literary agents · for an author querying a book
LIVE

A web tool that helps an author find and track literary agents. The dashboard shows the share of open agents queried so far, and keeps open agents, replies still waiting, full requests and rejections apart.

  • Query next: the best-fit open agents who have not been queried yet.
  • Follow up: a reminder when a query has had no reply for 6 weeks. Most agents treat 6 to 12 weeks of silence as a pass.
  • New from Monday's scan: agents added or reopened by the weekly scan.
  • Closed right now: agents who are not taking queries, kept on the list so they can be caught when they reopen.
